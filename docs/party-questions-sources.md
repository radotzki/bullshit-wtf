# Sources: party night questions

Not shown to players. One entry per question of `functions/data/party-questions.json`: the truth, the page in its `citation` field, and what that page says. Every page was read on 19 November 2026 with a page reader. The reader quotes at most about 125 characters at a time, so a long sentence may appear as exact pieces joined with "...". Each house lie was checked against the same page so that none is also true.

Written for a table of adults at a bachelorette or bachelor party, a grown-up birthday, a weekend away or a night out: weddings and pre-wedding customs, birthdays, trips and hotels, drinks and nightlife. Adult but not explicit, nothing about minors, nothing unkind about a living person, and no fact that changes from year to year (no current records, prices or rankings). The Hebrew questions are written in Hebrew. They are their own facts; only the daiquiri appears in both languages, from two sides (the name's iron mine in English, its inventor in Hebrew). Hebrew Wikipedia did not load in the page reader, so Hebrew questions cite English Wikipedia or another named publisher.

Worth a second look in review:
- en: the Irish coffee origin is "one version" on its page; the question says so. The Danish pepper custom cites Atlas Obscura. The Chic question's house lies are other real Chic songs. The happy hour quote names the crew of USS Arkansas, a US Navy ship.
- he: the Dahab question says "some scholars identify", since the identification is disputed. The Finnish "bath broom" is written "מטאטא סאונה". The Negroni, Zakynthos ship and daiquiri questions hedge as their pages do. The extra candle "for the following year" is what the page says of Israel. The right index finger at the chuppah may be known to many Israelis.

Dropped while checking (not in the file): the French "burial of boyhood" (too easy), the 1896 Seeley dinner in English (its date and city came from a summary, not a quote; the Hebrew one quotes the page), Korean rice cookers, the Welsh lovespoon wheel, Prince Leopold's edible cake, the Hoop Dreams fee, Queen's Day, "Notes and Queries", Japan's two-hour reception (an industry habit that may change), and the Hebrew telling of facts the English set already has (the motel's name, Thomas Cook's first trip, the Tom Collins hoax, the Moscow mule photos, Irish coffee, karaoke's inventor, Bertha Benz's trip, Riga's police unit, the Danish cake figure and the candles' bacteria).
