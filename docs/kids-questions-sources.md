# Sources: kid-friendly questions

Not shown to players. One entry per question of `functions/data/kids-questions.json`: the truth, the page in its `citation` field, and what that page says. Every page was read on 18 November 2026. Each house lie was checked against the same page so that none is also true.

Written for a table of children of about 8 to 12 playing with adults: animals, the body, space, food, sport and everyday things, with nothing rude, scary or adult. The Hebrew questions are written in Hebrew, about different facts from the English ones, not translated.

**en-01. books** · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_tennis  
When people first played table tennis in Victorian England, they used a row of ____ as the net  
House lies: chairs, walking sticks, umbrellas  
Source says: "A row of books stood upright along the centre of the table as a net, two more books served as rackets."

**en-02. 4 am** · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alarm_clock  
The first American alarm clock, made in 1787 by Levi Hutchins, could only ring at ____  
House lies: midnight, 7 am, noon  
Source says: "The first American alarm clock was created in 1787 by Levi Hutchins"; it "only rang at 4 am, to wake him for his job."

**en-03. dentist** · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton_candy  
Cotton candy machines were invented in 1897 by a candy maker and a ____  
House lies: clown, barber, firefighter  
Source says: "Machine-spun cotton candy was invented in 1897 by dentist William Morrison and confectioner John C. Wharton."

**en-04. pig hair** · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toothbrush  
The first toothbrushes with bristles, made in China over a thousand years ago, used ____  
House lies: horse tails, goose feathers, grass roots  
Source says: The first bristle toothbrush, Tang dynasty (619-907), used "hog bristles".

**en-05. sideways slits** · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goat  
Our pupils are round, but a goat's pupils are ____  
House lies: star-shaped, heart-shaped, triangles  
Source says: Goats have "horizontal, slit-shaped pupils". Kept out of the lies: rectangles and squares (the shape is often described so).

**en-06. puggle** · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Echidna  
A baby echidna is called a ____  
House lies: pinlet, spikelet, joey-poo  
Source says: A baby echidna is called a "puggle". Kept out of the lies: anything said of the platypus.

**en-07. black** · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_bear  
Under its white fur, a polar bear's skin is ____  
House lies: pink, bright blue, see-through  
Source says: "The skin, including the nose and lips, is black and absorbs heat."

**en-08. dad** · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seahorse  
Baby seahorses are carried in a pouch and born from their ____  
House lies: mum, big sister, grandma  
Source says: "The male seahorse is equipped with a brood pouch"; "When the fry are ready to be born, the male expels them".

**en-09. 1.3 million** · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun  
You could fit about ____ Earths inside the Sun  
House lies: 5,000, 300, 20 billion  
Source says: Infobox: "Volume ... 1,300,000 × Earth volume".

**en-10. storm** · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Red_Spot  
Jupiter's Great Red Spot is a giant ____ that is wider than the whole Earth  
House lies: volcano, lake of lava, mountain  
Source says: In 2017 the spot was "1.3 times the diameter of Earth" (three times in 2004); it is an anticyclonic storm.

**en-11. purple** · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrot  
About a thousand years ago, carrots grown in Asia and Europe were ____  
House lies: blue, green, grey  
Source says: In the 10th century "roots from West Asia, India and Europe were purple". Kept out of the lies: yellow, white, red, black (all real carrot colours).

**en-12. forever** · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honey  
Kept in a closed jar, honey can last ____  
House lies: about a week, exactly one year, ten days  
Source says: Honey "has an indefinite shelf life"; "when properly stored, honey therefore does not spoil". The same page says no edible honey was found in Egyptian tombs, so the question does not claim it.

**en-13. ear** · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stapes  
The smallest bone in your body is in your ____  
House lies: little toe, nose, pinky finger  
Source says: The stapes is "the smallest and lightest bone in the human body", one of the ossicles of the middle ear.

**en-14. 1.5 cm** · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_height  
Children are about ____ shorter by late afternoon than when they wake up  
House lies: 3 mm, 5 cm, 10 cm  
Source says: A study found "a mean decrease of 1.54 centimetres (0.6 in) in the heights of 100 children" from morning to late afternoon.

**en-15. animal bladders** · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ball_(association_football)  
The first footballs were blown-up ____  
House lies: fish skins, coconut shells, rubber boots  
Source says: Early footballs were "made out of inflated animal bladder, with later leather coverings". Kept out of the lies: leather and cork.

**en-16. peas and beans** · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanut  
Peanuts are not really nuts: they belong to the same plant family as ____  
House lies: potatoes, sunflowers, coconuts  
Source says: Peanuts are "botanically ... not nuts"; family Fabaceae, "commonly known as the legume, bean, or pea family".

**en-17. radioactive** · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banana_equivalent_dose  
Bananas are very slightly ____  
House lies: magnetic, fizzy, see-through  
Source says: "Bananas contain naturally occurring radioactive isotopes, particularly potassium-40."

**en-18. skull** · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodpecker  
A woodpecker's tongue bone wraps all the way around its ____  
House lies: beak, neck, heart  
Source says: The hyoid "winds around outside the skull" and "wraps around the brain case". Kept out of the lies: brain.

**en-19. one eye open** · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unihemispheric_slow-wave_sleep  
Some dolphins rest while swimming in a group, and each one keeps ____  
House lies: its mouth open, its tail still, its fins touching  
Source says: Of resting Pacific white-sided dolphins: "Dolphins swimming on the left side of the pod have their right eyes open while dolphins swimming on the right side of the pod have their left eyes open."

**en-20. an hour and a half** · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Space_Station  
The International Space Station goes all the way around the Earth in about ____  
House lies: a whole day, a week, ten minutes  
Source says: One orbit "roughly every 93 minutes around Earth, 15.5 times a day."

**he-01. עגורים** · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hula_Valley  
בכל חורף עוצרים באגם החולה עשרות אלפי ____ בדרכם מפינלנד לאתיופיה, והחקלאים מאכילים אותם כדי שלא יאכלו את השדות

**he-02. פי שניים** · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chameleon

לשפן הסלע הקטן, שחי בעין גדי, יש שיניים קדמיות שמזכירות את אלה של ____, שהוא קרוב משפחה רחוק שלו
